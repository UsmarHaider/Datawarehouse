## Week 1 — Introduction to DW & BI

Starts from the **Superstore problem**: 50 branches, millions of sales a day, and one CEO question — *which product categories lost money this week?* Running that report on the live checkout database slows every billing counter in the country. The week answers why, and what to build instead.

| Task | Covers | Data |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The messy sales log — find six faults, assign each an owner, compute the real total | `DWBI_W1_Messy_Sales_Log.csv` |
| 2 | OLTP vs OLAP — classify eight scenarios, write your own, argue a borderline case | — |
| 3 | No history, no single truth — Inmon's properties under pressure | — |
| 4 | Place eight items across Source → ETL → Presentation → BI Apps | — |
| 5 | **Hands-on:** connect, relate, slice, validate | `DWBI_W1_Regional_Sales.xlsx` |

Both datasets are generated by [`week1/assignments/generate_week1_data.py`](week1/assignments/generate_week1_data.py). The messy log carries **eight deliberately planted faults** — four date formats, one branch spelled four ways, a duplicate row, amounts stored as text, a return as a negative, and a decimal-point error that charges ten times the real price. Details in [`week1/README.md`](week1/README.md).

## Week 2 — DW Lifecycle & Architecture

Runs on **PakFreight**, a freight company that moves cargo by marine, rail and road. Each mode keeps its own file in its own format. Three teams, three totals, zero trust.

| Task | Covers |
|---|---|
| 1 | The three tiers, Inmon's four properties, EDW vs ODS, independent vs dependent marts |
| 2 | Inmon vs Kimball, both architectures drawn, ten scenarios, the lifecycle, SDLC vs CLDS |
| 3 | **Data Detective** — profile the raw files, reproduce the four totals, trace the lineage |
| 4 | **Hands-on:** staging → storage → presentation in Power BI |
| 5 | Latency, schema drift, lineage, BI roles, responsibility map |

### The one number the week is built around

Three mismatched files become one table: **34 rows, 578,650 kg.**

| Source | Raw | Removed | Loaded | Weight (kg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marine | 12 | 0 | 12 | 206,500 |
| Rail | 10 | 0 | 10 | 295,250 |
| Road | 15 | 3 | 12 | 76,900 |
| **Total** | **37** | **3** | **34** | **578,650** |

Every figure in the Week 2 brief — row counts, weights, status counts, tonnes by mode — was reproduced from the source files and verified before the brief was written.

### Why three teams get three answers

| Who | Answer | What they did |
|---|---|---|
| Operations | 661.65 t | kept the TOTAL row |
| Finance | 283.70 t | read the rail tons as kilograms |
| Sales | 584.75 t | kept the duplicate |
| **One staging area** | **578.65 t** | **the true number** |

Nobody is lying and nobody has miscounted. Task 3 makes students reproduce each wrong figure arithmetically, which is a far better argument for a staging tier than any slide.

## A note on teacher material

The Data Detective **teacher key** is deliberately not in this repository. `.gitignore` blocks anything matching `*TEACHER*`, `*_KEY*` or `*ANSWER*`, so it cannot be committed here by accident. Distribute it separately.
